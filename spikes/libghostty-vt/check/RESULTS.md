# vtcheck results: libghostty-vt against TBD's SwiftTerm fork

This is the headless engine check for the question in `../README.md`. "S" is
TBD's SwiftTerm fork and "G" is stock libghostty-vt. A direction such as "S to G"
means the writer on the S side produced the bytes and a fresh G parsed them.

## The answer

- **S to G holds.** TBD's unmodified `TerminalSnapshotWriter` output, parsed by a
  fresh G, gives the same screen as the same bytes parsed by a fresh S in 211 of
  213 states. The other 2 are grapheme differences that also appear when both
  engines read the raw stream. No difference category is new to the crossing. In
  47 states G matches the source and the S control does not, because G keeps a
  hidden cursor hidden.
- **The doc's "both sides or neither" claim does not survive as a claim about the
  format.** A viewer on G can attach through today's preamble, with one change:
  reset a reused G first. Detach, G to S, keeps the screen in 210 of 213 states. It
  needs a new writer on the G side and one ordering fix. This is byte-level
  evidence; the live attach and detach flow did not run. See [Handover](#handover).
- **The control is not clean.** With TBD's code on both ends and no Ghostty
  involved, S to S fails the screen in 26 of 213 states and loses a hidden cursor in
  47. See [Defects TBD has today](#defects-tbd-has-today).
- **Speed** – G is 1.1 to 6.3 times faster across all ten workloads, and about 4 times on real Claude bytes.
- **Memory** – G uses half of S's memory for idle terminals and about 15% less for
  one full scrollback, holding up to 1.8% fewer rows. A host-called compress takes
  that scrollback to 0.7 to 1.9 MB.
- **Side's 0/60 unicode check is a comparison artifact.** It reproduces on TBD's
  fork as 0/60, and one change to the reader makes it 60/60.

- **libghostty-vt** – 1.0.0, the official xcframework from
  `https://release.files.ghostty.org/libghostty-vt/1.0.0/ghostty-vt-1.0.0.xcframework.zip`,
  SHA-256 `2c32b7409b2b6c2ef6cfd606526de357762ad458bdc27134ad074e86506a8bc2`, checked

- **SwiftTerm** – `cheapsteak/SwiftTerm` at
  `c6b544f84bf36fd81cd88bac356b27897fc6a2ab`. Facts about it come from this
  package's own `.build/checkouts`.
- **TBD** – commit `5891dd29470073ceb1002f7c45a065b58183e2fa`. The files in
  `Sources/TBDTerminalSerialization/` that the check uses are symlinks to the real
  ones, unmodified. `TerminalScreenProjection.swift` is left out.
- **Machine** – Apple M4 Pro, 12 cores, 48 GB, macOS 27.0.1, Swift 6.2.4, Xcode 26.3,
  Python 3.14.6. The box is shared, and other work ran throughout.
- **Corpus** – `../workloads/`, git-ignored, regenerated by `vtcheck corpus`.
  - **Synthetic, 200x60** – ascii 36,011,907 bytes, colors 33,555,096, unicode
    16,777,385, cursor 16,777,230, tui 16,777,799, scroll 16,777,223.
  - **Recorded** – `rec-claude` 21,403 bytes and `rec-claude-classic` 68,120 at
    120x40, `rec-vim` 30,282 and `rec-less` 25,018 at 100x30.
  - **How the Claude recordings were made** – `claude` 2.1.319 through
    `scripts/claude-stub.py`, so no tokens were spent, in a pty, from a throwaway
    directory, for three stub turns. `rec-claude` is the fullscreen renderer on the
    alternate screen. `rec-claude-classic` sets
    `CLAUDE_CODE_DISABLE_ALTERNATE_SCREEN=1`.

Engine settings, in every subcommand:

- **Scrollback** – `GHOSTTY_TERMINAL_OPT_SCROLLBACK_MAX_BYTES` is SIZE_MAX, set
  first, because the 1.0.0 default is a 10,000-byte cap that wins over any line
  limit. `GHOSTTY_TERMINAL_OPT_SCROLLBACK_MAX_LINES` is then 10,000 for `bench`,
  `memory` and `diff`, and 5,000 for `handover`, which is TBD's value
  (`HolderReader.scrollbackLines`, `HolderHandback.handbackScrollbackLines`).
- **Grapheme clustering** – mode 2027 is on through
  `GHOSTTY_TERMINAL_OPT_MODE_DEFAULT`, as Ghostty.app does. S has no equivalent
  mode: DECRQM 2027 answers 0, and its clustering is unconditional. With 2027 off, G
  disagrees with S on emoji, including in the real Claude recordings (21 cells of
  text in `rec-claude`, 33 in `rec-claude-classic`). With it on, those go away, so on
  is the setting closest to S.
- **Locking** – S is fed and read under `terminalLock`, the way the daemon does it.

## Commands

Build first, from the repo root:

```
scripts/swift-safe build -c release --package-path spikes/libghostty-vt/check
```

The bench and memory figures below come from a run with nothing else from this spike
going. Other sessions on the box were still working, at load 10 to 13. Re-run them
from the repo root with:

```
spikes/libghostty-vt/check/.build/release/vtcheck bench
spikes/libghostty-vt/check/.build/release/vtcheck memory
```

The other subcommands, from the same directory:

- `vtcheck corpus <scratch-dir>` regenerates `../workloads/`.
- `vtcheck bench [--runs N] [workload...]` runs at least 5, 7 by default.
- `vtcheck diff [--small] [--no-grapheme] [name...]`: `--small` skips the six
  synthetic streams. A full run takes about 3 minutes.
- `vtcheck handover [name-substring]`
- `vtcheck signals`
- `vtcheck sidecheck` repeats Side's row check.
- `vtcheck tabs` measures where a tab lands past column 80.
- `vtcheck show <scenario | workload@offset | escaped-bytes>` shows one state in
  both engines and every direction, with the bytes each writer produced.

JSON goes to `../out/`, which is git-ignored.

## Diff

The same bytes went through both engines, compared at up to 32 checkpoints in each
stream, each at parser ground. History rows are compared at the end only, aligned at
the newest row. That covers 432 checkpoints across 81 named scenarios, 4 recordings
and 6 synthetic streams. All 22 compared modes were answered by both engines.

Most of the corpus agrees. `scroll`, `tui` and `rec-claude-classic` show no
difference in any visible category. `ascii` and `unicode` differ only in retained row
count, so 16 MiB of `unicode` matches at cell level.

Rulings cite Ghostty's tests in the vt-1.0.0 sources, ECMA-48 and the fork's code.
Neither `xterm` nor `vttest` was available, so a ruling that cites xterm rests on
Ghostty's own statement about it.

- **Colour lost after 65,535 distinct styles** – S is wrong. In `colors`, from about
  2 MiB in, S lost foreground colour on 254,744 cells on screen and 1,376,273 in
  history, plus 9 backgrounds on screen, 32 in history, and the pen at 29 checkpoints.
  - **Cause:** `CellArena.intern(attribute:)` in `CellStorage.swift` returns nil once
    its table is full, and the callers fall back with `?? 0`, the default style. No
    reclaim path turned up in the fork.
  - **Reproducer:** any stream with more than 65,535 distinct attribute values. The
    minimiser could not shrink it, because the count is the cause.
- **Tab past column 80, on a terminal built wider than 80** – S is wrong. On a
  terminal built at 120 or 200 columns, a tab from column 88 goes to the last column,
  119 or 199, on both screens. One built at 80 and resized lands on 96, and so does G.
  - **Cause:** `Terminal.init` grows its buffers through `setup()`, and
    `Buffer.resize` extends the tab-stop array with `false`. Only
    `Terminal.resize(cols:rows:)` calls `setupTabStops`.
  - **Reproducer:** `vtcheck tabs`. No stream in the `diff` corpus tabs that far. The
    G to S tab probe found it.
- **Erase at pending wrap** – S is wrong. Reproducer: `\e[1;198Hxyz\e[K` at 200
  columns. G blanks the `z`, and S keeps it: 3 cells on screen and 1 in history, in
  `cursor`. S represents pending wrap as `x == cols`, so EL starts past the last cell.
  Ghostty's test "eraseLine resets pending wrap" pins its behaviour.
- **Printing over half a wide cell** – S is wrong. Reproducers: `字\e[1;2Hx` and
  `漢字\e[1;3Hx`. S leaves a width-2 head with no tail, or a tail with no head. G
  clears the orphan half, and "print over wide spacer tail" pins that.
- **Overline** – S is wrong. Reproducer: `\e[53mo`. S has no overline attribute and
  logs "Unknown SGR attribute: 53" to stdout. ECMA-48 defines SGR 53.
- **`?25l` then `?1049h`** – S is probably wrong. S turns the cursor on when entering
  an alternate screen, in `showCursor()` under the `?47`, `?1047` and `?1049` cases. G
  leaves visibility alone, and Ghostty's source says its screen switch is based on
  xterm's `charproc.c`. This quirk drives the largest handover category below.
- **Devanagari conjunct, Thai and similar clusters** – Could not tell which suits real
  programs. In `combining-marks`, S stores `क्` and `ष` as two narrow cells, and G stores
  `क्ष` as one wide cell, per "print Devanagari grapheme should be wide". The cursor ends
  in the same place on both. This disappears with 2027 off. Which is right for a given
  TUI depends on that TUI's own width calculation.
- **VS16 at the right edge** – Could not tell which suits real programs. For 79 `a`
  then `❤` U+FE0F on 80 columns, S keeps a narrow cell at column 79, and G moves a wide
  cell to the next row, per "VS16 to make wide character on next line". The cursor
  differs by 2 columns. This disappears with 2027 off.
- **`CSI S` on the primary screen** – Probably G, on one side's evidence. Reproducer:
  `\e[3S`. G pushes rows to scrollback, and S does not. "scrollUp creates scrollback in
  primary screen" says this is like xterm. ECMA-48 has no scrollback to speak of.
- **Soft-wrap flag after a line is shifted** – Could not tell. 310 rows, in `rec-vim`,
  `rec-less` and `cursor`. Reproducer: wrap a line, then `\e[H\eM`. G clears the flags
  on rows that IL, DL or RI move, as "insertLines resets wrap" requires. S keeps them.
  No spec covers this. It never changes the screen, only resize reflow and selection
  joining.
- **Mouse mode bits** – Could not tell. 31 checkpoints in `rec-claude`. After
  `?1000h ?1002h`, S holds one value and reports only 1002, and G holds both bits.
  Effective tracking agrees while a program only raises the level, which is all the
  recordings do.
- **Inert right margin** – No effect found. 93 checkpoints. S's alternate buffer
  reports right margin 79 at any width until its first clear. It does nothing while
  DECLRMM is off, so it is reported apart. DECLRMM on at another width was not tested.
- **Retained row count** – Expected. S trims per line and G per page.
- **Style on blank cells** – Cosmetic, and reported apart, like never-written against
  space. A foreground colour on a space accounts for 55,265 cells on screen and
  298,890 in history, all in `colors`, from the style cap above.

### S to G: holds

- **Nothing new at the crossing.** The check for a difference present in S to G and
  absent from both the S to S control and that state's engine baseline found none.
- **Compared with S to S directly.** The G receiver's screen differs from the S
  receiver's in 2 of 213 states, `combining-marks` and `vs16-at-edge`, both engine
  differences above. Cursor visibility differs in 47, where G matches the source. The
  rest is the cosmetic inert margin.
- **The 28 states that fail are accounted for.** 19 are the row shift, 3 are
  background-only cells, 2 are hyperlinks, 2 are S's own half-wide cells, and 2 are the
  grapheme states. The first 26 fail S to S the same way.
- **Probes track the control.** Print, tab, scroll and stream sit within 3 states of S
  to S, and restore cursor is ahead.
- **Leave-alt is the exception, and it is mode bits.** S to G's 28 misses are 23 states
  of mouse bits with the same effective tracking, 3 of row shift, 1 of DECOM and
  `hidden-then-alt`. S to S's 6 misses are the same 3 and DECOM, plus 2 of DECAWM.
- **The real stream continues correctly.** After the preamble, 64 KiB more of each
  recording leaves S to G level with S to S at 125 of 128. The 3 misses are the row
  shift, in `rec-claude-classic`, and they are identical in both directions.

### S to G into a used receiver: holds after a reset

The reset prelude does not fully supersede a reused G. Each row compares the result
with the same preamble into a fresh receiver, in states with no visible-category
difference:

| prior life of the receiver | S to S | S to G | S to S, RIS first | S to G, RIS first |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| vim-like | 213/213 | 135/213 | 135/213 | 213/213 |
| everything the prelude never names | 0/213 | 0/213 | 213/213 | 213/213 |

- **Vim-like** – all 78 alternate states fail on G with a stale background. Ghostty's
  `?1049h` erases using the alternate screen's own leftover pen before it copies the
  primary cursor across. The writer's `ESC[0m` only reached the primary. Cells the
  preamble does not rewrite keep that background.
- **Everything** – S and G both fail, because the prelude leaves charsets, kitty flags,
  left and right margins, reverse video, LNM, sync output, alternate scroll 1007 and an
  open hyperlink in place. On G, mouse encodings 1005, 1015 and 1016 also survive,
  because they are separate bits there and `?1006l` clears only its own.
- **Reset first** – feeding RIS ahead of the preamble makes G correct in all 213 states
  on both prior lives. `ghostty_terminal_reset()` is the host-side form.
- **Do not copy that to S.** RIS leaves S's stale alternate content in place, because
  its reset path activates the normal buffer with `clearAlt: false`. The later `?1049h`
  shows it again, which is why S with RIS scores 135/213 on the vim-like case.

### G to S: holds, with a new writer and one ordering fix

A G viewer must hand a preamble back on detach, so this is the other half of moving
the viewer first.

- **Screen** – 210 of 213. The 3 misses are `combining-marks`, `vs16-at-edge` and
  `overline`, all engine differences. S cannot hold what G held in those states.
- **Cursor visibility** – fails in 48 states, for the S quirk above: the formatter emits
  `?25l` before `?1049h`, and S turns the cursor back on. A writer needs to emit `?25l`
  after `?1049h`. TBD's own writer has the same defect.
- **Formatter scope** – it covers the active screen's whole page list, scrollback
  included, which answers the question left open in
  `docs/research/2026-10-28-terminal-engine-libghostty.md`. It covers only the active
  screen, so on an alternate screen the primary and its scrollback are absent. The
  leave-alt probe scores 70 of 78 on that bare output.
- **Working route for alternate screens** – clone through the snapshot API, feed
  `?1049l` to the clone, format it with cursor, style, protection and charset extras,
  then append the bare formatter output. That scores 77 of 78 on the leave-alt probe on
  both S and G receivers, and leaves the live terminal untouched. It uses the snapshot
  API for the in-process copy only, so no snapshot bytes cross a build. This
  composition belongs to this harness, not to TBD or Ghostty.
- **Reverse wrap** – `?45h` after `?7l` is refused by this fork, as the comment in TBD's
  writer records, and the formatter emits them in that order. 1 state.
- **Mouse downgrade** – after `?1003h ?1000h`, a bare motion reports nothing on the
  source but reports on a formatter replay. The formatter writes the bits in mode order,
  not last-set order, so the replay ends at any-motion. This affects G to G as well, and
  the mode-bit comparison cannot see it, so `vtcheck signals` measures it through the
  mouse encoder. Programs that only raise the level are unaffected.
- **DECOM across a leave-alt** – set inside the alternate screen, it stays set after
  leaving on every VT path, which is the 1 miss in 77 of 78. The binary snapshot does
  not have this.
- **Why the probe column sits below G to G** – cursor visibility in 47 states, soft-wrap
  flags the engines keep differently in 21, and the tab-stop bug above in 26. In those
  26, the receiver holds the correct stops from the formatter, and the raw S terminal it
  is compared with is the wrong one.

### G to G

- **Formatter VT** – 213 of 213 and level on every probe, apart from 3 restore-cursor
  states, because the saved cursor is not carried. It needs every extra except palette,
  which is left out so a replay does not overwrite the theme, and `unwrap` true and
  `trim` false, so soft wraps and trailing rows survive.
- **Binary snapshot, for information only** – 213 of 213, and level on every probe
  including restore-cursor and leave-alt. Its format is outside the ABI promise.

## Defects TBD has today

All of these are measured with no Ghostty on the receiving end, or read from the fork,
and all appear to be worth their own bug report. Nothing here was changed.

- **Row shift when the viewport ends in blank rows and history exists.** 19 states,
  including 6 of 24 checkpoints in the real `claude` classic recording.
  - **Cause:** `TerminalCellWalk.lines` drops every trailing blank row, on the reasoning
    that a viewer's screen is already blank there. With history present, the replay then
    scrolls that many rows too few. The text lands lower than in the source, history
    comes up short by the same count, and the absolute cursor address points at the
    wrong line. Its comment says "below the cursor", but the loop never looks at the
    cursor.
  - **Reproducers:** `primary-cleared-after-scrollback` is `\e[H\e[2J`, the `clear`
    capability in xterm's terminfo entry, with scrollback kept. 23 rows shift. Thirty
    lines ending in `\r\n` on a 24-row grid shift by 1, and the cursor lands on the last
    line of text, where the next output overwrites it.
  - **Tests:** no name in `Tests/TBDAppTests/TerminalSnapshotRoundTripTests.swift`
    mentions blank rows, clearing or erasing.
- **Background-only cells are lost.** Erasing with a background colour (BCE) leaves
  cells with code 0, and `render` stops at `getTrimmedLength()`, which trims them. The
  comment there says a coloured blank keeps its background, which holds only for blanks
  before the last character. `bce-erase` loses 1,916 cells, and a coloured alternate
  screen loses 1,919.
- **A hidden cursor comes back on an alternate screen.** 47 states. The writer emits
  `?25l` before `?1049h`, and S's `?1049h` shows the cursor.
- **Tab stops past column 80.** See [Diff](#diff). The daemon builds its terminal
  through `TerminalOptions` at the real width (`HolderReader.swift:1415`), which is the
  affected route, and SwiftTerm's view does the same when it has a size at creation. So
  the daemon's model is expected to misplace tabbed text past column 80 on wide
  terminals. That is inferred from the code and `vtcheck tabs`, not observed in a live
  session.
- **Colour loss after 65,535 styles and erase at pending wrap.** See [Diff](#diff).
  Whether RIS resets the style table was not tested.
- **State the writer never carries**, with the states affected in the S to S column.
  These bound what any receiver can show:
  - pen, 42
  - pending wrap, 3
  - OSC 8 hyperlinks, 2
  - kitty keyboard flags, reverse video, LNM, sync output, DECLRMM margins, and mouse
    modes 9, 1005, 1015 and 1016, 1 each
  - charsets, tab stops and primary DECSC, seen through the probes

| scroll | 55/55 | 55/55 | 55/55 |
| tui | 58/58 | 58/58 | 58/58 |
| unicode | 0/60 | 60/60 | 60/60 |

It is a comparison artifact. `getCharacter(col:row:)` returns NUL for a wide cell's
trailing half, the check turns NUL into a space, and each wide glyph gains a space that
plain-text formatter output does not have. Skipping cells of width 0 is the only change
needed. The cell-level `diff` agrees, with no text or width difference anywhere in 16
MiB of `unicode`.

## Signals

`vtcheck signals` feeds each sequence to both engines with every effect callback wired.
Everything below was observed unless marked otherwise. Real wire forms come from the
recordings: Claude sends `OSC 7501;state=working:app=claude-code`.

Sequences TBD uses, and what G gives:

- **OSC 7501 report** – yes, `GHOSTTY_TERMINAL_OPT_PROGRAM_STATUS`, called inside
  `vt_write`. It delivers parsed state, kind, progress, id, app and a decoded title.
- **OSC 7501 probe** – yes, answered through `WRITE_PTY` during parsing, which is the
  timing TBD needs, because Claude queues the probe ahead of its DA1 query. The reply is
  richer than TBD's:
  `?:states=idle,working,done,blocked,error:kinds=permission,question,auth`. Real
  `claude` 2.1.319 sent the same reports with either reply and none with no reply, so
  the check discriminates. The callback must be set for G to answer.
- **OSC 777 notify** – yes, `DESKTOP_NOTIFICATION`, title and body.
- **OSC 0 and OSC 2 title** – yes, `TITLE_CHANGED`, then read `DATA_TITLE`. TBD's
  handler is empty today.
- **OSC 7 pwd** – yes, `PWD_CHANGED`, then read `DATA_PWD`. TBD's handler is empty today.
- **OSC 8 hyperlink** – yes, per cell through `ghostty_grid_ref_hyperlink_uri`, read when
  clicked. Neither engine has a callback.
- **OSC 52 write** – yes, `CLIPBOARD_WRITE`, decoded, with MIME type.
- **OSC 52 read** – yes, `CLIPBOARD_READ`, answered through a reply on the request. A
  callback that does not reply produces an empty OSC 52 answer.
- **BEL** – yes, `BELL`.
- **DECRQM** – yes, answered automatically. `DATA_MODE` reads any mode directly, so
  TBD's `modeProbe` round trip would not be needed. Two answers differ: 1049 is 0 on S
  and 2 on G, and 2027 is 0 on S and 1 on G.
- **DA1, DA2, DA3, XTVERSION, size reports** – yes, through `DEVICE_ATTRIBUTES`,
  `XTVERSION` and `SIZE`, so the host chooses the answers. S replied
  `?65;4;1;2;6;21;22;17;28c` to DA1 and `SwiftTerm+c6b544f84bf3` to XTVERSION, and
  answered no DA3. TBD would have to decide what G advertises.
- **DSR 5, CPR, `?6n`, `?996n`** – yes, same replies as S.
- **Kitty keyboard flags** – yes, answered, and read with `DATA_KITTY_KEYBOARD_FLAGS`.
- **Synchronized output** – yes, `RENDER_HOLD(true/false)`.
- **OSC 10 and 11 queries** – only when the host sets `OPT_COLOR_FOREGROUND` and
  `OPT_COLOR_BACKGROUND`. Unset, G stays silent. With them set, the replies are correct.
  S answers from its theme.
- **Resize request `CSI 8 t`** – yes, `RESIZE_WINDOW`.

State TBD reads from SwiftTerm, from a grep of `Sources/TBDApp` and `Sources/TBDDaemon`
(`Sources/TBDDaemonLib` does not exist):

- **`mouseMode`** – `DATA_MOUSE_TRACKING`, plus `ghostty_mouse_encoder_set_from_terminal`
  for what `encodeButton` and `sendEvent` do today.
- **`isCurrentBufferAlternate`** – `DATA_ACTIVE_SCREEN`.
- **`bracketedPasteMode`, `applicationCursor`, the DECRQM mode probe** – `DATA_MODE`.
- **`buffer.x`, `buffer.y`** – `DATA_CURSOR_X`, `DATA_CURSOR_Y`.
- **`showCursor`, `hideCursor` delegate calls** – no callback. Read
  `DATA_CURSOR_VISIBLE`.
- **`getLine`, `getScrollInvariantLine`** – grid refs at active and history points.
- **`translateToString`** – formatter in plain mode.
- **`requestOpenLink`** – view gesture plus the hyperlink read above.
- **`sendResponse`** – not needed for the probe, which G answers itself.
- **`setCursorStyle`** – `OPT_DEFAULT_CURSOR_STYLE`. `CSI q` reaches no callback, and the
  render state carries it (header read, not exercised here).
- **`buffer.scrollTop`, `scrollBottom`** – **no getter.** This check reads them by
  parsing the tail of a formatter run. Only a port of TBD's writer would need them.
- **`buffer.savedX`, `savedY`** – **no getter.** Only a port of TBD's writer would need
  them.
- **`totalLinesTrimmed`** – **no equivalent.** `DATA_SCROLLBACK_ROWS` and
  `DATA_TOTAL_ROWS` give counts. TBD's cell walk and screen projection use it to
  enumerate rows, which is not how G would be read.
- **`terminalLock`** – the host brings its own. The header states the terminal is not
  thread-safe.

Sequences that reach S and not G, none of which TBD handles:

- OSC 1 icon title
- OSC 6 document
- OSC 4 palette change notice
- `CSI 21 t`, which is off by default in G

Sequences that reach G and not S: OSC 9 notify, OSC 1337 CurrentDir, OSC 133 semantic
prompts, ENQ. On RIS, G also fires `PROGRESS_REPORT`, `PROGRAM_STATUS` with state clear,
and `RESET`. OSC 9 as a notification is a new surface a host would have to decide about.

## What could not be tested

- **Live flow.** This is a byte-level check. Attach, detach and resize through TBD's real
  daemon and app did not run. `../view/` is a window of its own, not TBD's handover.
- **Resize across a handover, and reflow.** Not in the state set. The soft-wrap
  difference above is where it would show.
- **Scrollback viewport position.** Not modelled in the comparison.
- **Kitty graphics and sixel.** Not in the corpus.
- **The app's mode reader.** The delegate here has the daemon's shape. The app's
  `RecordedModeReplies` path did not run. `resetPrelude` is internal, so only the whole
  writer was exercised.
- **Long real sessions.** The recordings are three stub turns of 21 to 68 KB. Claude sent
  no `?2026h` under this harness, so sync output is covered by a scenario only.
- **xterm and vttest.** Neither is installed, so xterm-based rulings rest on Ghostty's
  statements about it.
- **Whether real TUIs match G or S on conjuncts and edge VS16.**
- **Which route TBD's viewer takes for tab stops.** Its creation path depends on whether
  the view has a size at that moment, and that was not traced.
- **DECLRMM on at a non-80 alternate width.**
- **Reading compressed history.** Size only, no latency.
- **Thread and lock cost for G.** Everything here is single-threaded per terminal.
- **Timing quality.** `bench` and `memory` ran on a shared box with other sessions
  working, so ratios are the figures to trust.
